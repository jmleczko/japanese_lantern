ATtiny85-based design · touch-controlled, dual-channel (warm-white / red) dimmable LED lantern, with steady-color RGB charge status indicator

Reviewed and corrected based on feedback from an experienced electronics engineer The charge indicator was originally designed with a transistor oscillator for a blinking effect. That design used the TP4056's CHRG pin as a current return path beyond its intended ~5mA rating as a status output, and has been replaced with the simpler, standard steady-color arrangement TP4056 was actually designed around. Several other corrections and additions from that review are reflected below (PTC fuse, ISP header, 4.2V-basis resistor sizing, and an operating rule to keep the lantern off while charging).
Before connecting the battery: verify JST polarity JST-PH connector wiring isn't fully standardized across manufacturers — a mismatched battery and charge module can destroy the board the moment they're connected. Use a multimeter's DC voltage mode (not continuity mode, which is only for tracing unpowered wires) — a positive reading means the red probe is on battery positive. Before plugging in a battery you haven't used with this charger before:
  1. Check the battery listing's Q&A/reviews for anyone confirming compatibility with a TP4056-style module.
  2. Visually compare the battery's JST plug and the TP4056 module's socket side by side — red-to-red and black-to-black should line up at the same physical pin position.
  3. Confirm with DC voltage mode before connecting, rather than assuming.
Operating rule: keep the lantern off while charging A bare TP4056 module has no power-path management. If the LED load draws current while charging, the charger may never see current taper below its termination threshold, and can get stuck in charge mode or misreport status. Treat "off while charging" as a rule for this build, not an inconvenience — a small physical disconnect switch is the simplest way to enforce it.

The red lantern channel is drawn as a labeled placeholder — it is electrically identical to the white channel shown, just driven from PB1 instead of PB0, with its own R9–R11, LED2, and Q2.

Charging current: many bargain TP4056 modules ship configured for ~1A via a 1.2kΩ PROG resistor. In a confined wooden enclosure, swapping to a 2.4kΩ PROG resistor brings this down to ~500mA and meaningfully reduces heat — note this is a small SMD component you'd need to desolder and replace, not a simple setting.

Also confirm your battery and/or TP4056 module includes genuine over-discharge protection — ideally both a protected cell and a module with real protection circuitry, not charge-protection alone.
